*Goose Chaos in Pandemic* is a pixel-art game where you play a rebellious goose causing trouble under China's strict COVID-19 restrictions. It's also a memory archive, made for people who were there, and for those outside who want to understand what everyday life under those rules was really like.

## The Idea

I had this idea back when we were still under strict zero-COVID controls. In the second half of 2022, I took a PCR test every single day. Only with a negative test result could you go to work, buy groceries, or step into public places. If someone in your community tested positive, you might get quarantined at home, with a detector stuck to your door. Health codes, travel codes, daily swabs. The whole system reached into the smallest corners of daily life, deciding whether you could walk through a door.

Stuck inside, my friends and I played [Untitled Goose Game](https://goose.game) together, and at some point the joke started :"*If I were a goose, what would I do to break free from all these restrictions?*" Under that much pressure, all we wanted to do was cause trouble. So we came up with a ton of it. Snatch a test tube from the PCR booth. Walk into a mall without scanning a single code. Peck the detector off a quarantined door. 

I decided to build these absurd scenarios into my game. 

## Game Mechanics

The game runs on a simple loop. You wander a pixel-art city and walk up to a location, where a short dialogue explains what's going on (useful if you didn't live through it) before you choose how to cause trouble. Success depends on a dice roll: two dice per action, and the result decides whether the goose gets away with it and raises the chaos level, or gets caught. 

## Reflection

I tried to balance seriousness and playfulness in this game. The pandemic was a trauma for many people, and I did not want the experience of playing to feel heavy. Instead, I used rebellious and absurd elements to soften that weight, hoping to offer a little joy inside something bitter rather than sinking under it.

The game also works as a digital memory archive. Health codes, PCR testing, and quarantine formed our collective memory, and I did not want to leave it behind. 

Looking back, I think of what Foucault called the capillary form of power. During those months, technology quietly penetrated daily life and controlled it from within. Whether you could walk through a door depended on a health code. Whether you could travel to another province depended on a travel code. Control was woven into every ordinary act.
